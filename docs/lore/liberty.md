# 10924: Liberty

**10924: Liberty** is the current first-game direction for the 10924 browser RPG prototype.

The game centers on Via Four, the only surviving complete Via-class weapon from the Great War. It follows her creation, use, survival, classification, and eventual struggle to define herself beyond the word weapon.

## Core premise

Via Four was not created through destiny, selection, or clean scientific intent.

She was a child caught in war, misidentified during a medical crisis, infused with dedrix, and stabilized at an extreme saturation level. The result was a living person whose body could become a catastrophe-class armament.

The world calls her 119-04.

The war calls her necessary.

The state calls her terrorist.

The registry calls her Catastrophe 2A.

The game asks whether Via means weapon, survivor, person, or liberty.

## Game identity

- **Genre:** isometric narrative action RPG
- **Primary character:** Via Four
- **Signature mechanic:** deliberate dedrix transformation sequences
- **Primary conflict:** the Great War and its aftermath
- **Primary emotional arc:** a child weapon becoming a self
- **Primary question:** what shape do you take when everyone else has decided what you are?

## Tone

The story should be beautiful, violent, intimate, and morally complicated.

Neither the government nor the rebellion should be treated as wholly innocent.

The government pursued universal conquest, species eradication, chemical weapons, and Lumen mind inhibitors.

The rebellion resisted annihilation, but also employed child soldiers and committed atrocities in the name of survival.

Via Four exists in the wound between those truths.

## Intended player experience

The player should feel power and unease at the same time.

Via's transformations should be exciting, but never disconnected from their cost. Her strength should feel like something she survives, not just something she owns.

The player should gradually move from reacting to dedrix, to executing commands under pressure, to using transformation as an expression of Via's agency.
